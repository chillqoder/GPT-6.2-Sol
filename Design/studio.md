Build a website for a studio that sells vibe coding as a service — people come with an idea, a mood, a half-sentence scribbled on a napkin, and leave with working software that was conjured, not engineered.

This is a creativity test. There is no brief beyond that. Who the studio is, how it talks, what it believes, what the visitor sees, touches and feels — all of it is yours to invent. The site itself should feel like proof of the craft: if this studio can vibe something into existence, its own website is the first exhibit.

It must be creative and genuinely unusual. Not "a nice landing page with a twist" — a thing with its own logic, its own world, its own rules. Something that makes a person stop, poke at it, and send the link to a friend with "look at this". The structure, the layout, the way it moves, the way it reads — none of it should follow the shape you'd reach for by default. If you catch yourself building what a website is "supposed" to look like, stop and go somewhere stranger.

Hard rule: no AI slop. You know exactly what it looks like — violet-to-blue gradients, glowing blobs, glassmorphism cards, bento grids, sparkle and rocket emojis, Inter on a dark background, "Unleash the power of…", "Transform your ideas into reality", stock 3D shapes floating for no reason, copy that could belong to any company on earth. If a detail could appear on a thousand other AI-generated sites, it doesn't belong on this one. Every word and every pixel should feel chosen by someone with taste and an opinion.

Have a point of view. Take risks. Be weird on purpose, not by accident. Make it something no one has seen before — and if it reminds you of something you've seen a thousand times, throw it out and start over.
